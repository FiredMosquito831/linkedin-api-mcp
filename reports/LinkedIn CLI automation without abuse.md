# Official APIs leave most LinkedIn surfaces closed

No. Full control of every LinkedIn surface is not achievable without abuse, and the official routes cover a narrow slice. **Sign In with OpenID Connect and Share on LinkedIn are the only two products open to any developer**, and Share on LinkedIn grants `w_member_social` for creating posts (*verified*). Likes and comments appear under that open permission on two LinkedIn pages, but two other pages name an approval-gated scope for them (*verified; conflict unresolved*). Messaging and invitations need partner approval, and company-page management needs Community Management access that one page limits to registered legal organizations while another opens to "approved developers" (*verified; conflict unresolved*). Feed reads and direct-message reads have no official route for an individual (*verified*), and the notes found no official route for notifications, people search or profile edits (*absence unverified*). The unofficial routes to those surfaces are named as prohibited in the User Agreement and LinkedIn's help pages (*verified*), and the three existing projects that cover most surfaces reach them through cookies, browser automation or a vendor-hosted browser (*verified*). Even the official route is gray for scheduled posts and for a single command run from a CLI, because API Terms §3.1 item 26 bars using the APIs "to automate posting" and no LinkedIn text says whether a user-run command counts (*verified; unresolved*). The defensible build is a narrow, human-triggered CLI and importable library on official OAuth, with five open questions setting its ceiling. This is research on published terms, not legal advice, and the evidence is dated 2026-10-20.

*Reading the tags:* **verified** means checked against a primary page (LinkedIn or Microsoft Learn, an RFC, Node.js documentation, or an npm, PyPI or GitHub record) on 2026-10-20; the terms quotes were also checked against raw page text. **Secondhand** means a search summary, a Context7 index excerpt, a news or law-firm report, a vendor page, or a registry listing description. **Unverified** means not checked, an absence not established, or a conflict left open. An inference takes the status of its weakest premise, and design choices are labeled as design. Terms labels: **Allowed** (an explicit official permission covers the act and no quoted clause reads against it), **Gray** (a quoted clause could reach the act and LinkedIn has not settled it), **Prohibited** (a quoted clause names the act, or the only route is unofficial automation). Access labels: **Self-serve** (open products), **Restricted** (partner or vetted approval), **None** (closed scopes or no official endpoint).

## Sign-in and posting are open; likes and comments hang on one scope

The two open products are narrow by design. Sign In with OpenID Connect returns the member's name, photo and email (*verified*). [Share on LinkedIn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/consumer/integrations/self-serve/share-on-linkedin) says `w_member_social` is **"Required to create a LinkedIn post on behalf of the authenticated member"** (*verified*). [Getting Access](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/authentication/getting-access) adds that **"Open Permissions are the only permissions that are available to all developers without special approval"** (*verified*). Everything beyond those two products is restricted, closed or absent. The [Community Management overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/community-management-overview) calls its API a **"Vetted Product"** (*verified*), and the member read scope `r_member_social` is closed, with **"We're not accepting access requests at this time due to resource constraints"** (*verified*). The Profile, Connections and Messages APIs are restricted to developers or partners that LinkedIn approves (*verified*).

## LinkedIn names the unofficial routes, and item 26 leaves the official one open

LinkedIn's bans on unofficial routes are explicit. **UA §8.2 item 2** bars "software, devices, scripts, robots or any other means or processes" used "to scrape or copy the Services, including profiles and other data" ([User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)) (*verified*). **Item 13** bars "bots or other unauthorized automated methods" to access the Services, add or download contacts, send or redirect messages, comment on, like or share posts, or drive inauthentic engagement (*verified*). **API Terms §3.1 item 24** separately bars "scraping, crawling, spidering or using any other technology or software to access LinkedIn content outside the APIs" (*verified*). The help article [a1341387](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387) names crawlers, bots, browser plug-ins and browser extensions that "scrape, modify the appearance of, or automate activity on LinkedIn's website," and states that **"Any member who uses tools for such purposes is in violation of the User Agreement"** (*verified*). The Sales Navigator article [a1340567](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a1340567) adds that third-party software that automates tasks "could potentially export or scrape data," and that **"This is a violation of LinkedIn's User Agreement"** (*verified*). That page does not say whether "This" covers automation in general or only the export and scraping, so that reading is unresolved. Enforcement runs at the account level. **Some violations can lead to "permanent account restriction after a single violation,"** and the help page asks members to disable the tool before the account is re-enabled ([a1340522](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340522); [a1340567](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a1340567)) (*verified*). The official route does not shield the account: UA §9 permits restriction, and **API Terms §11.3 permits suspension "at any time for any reason"** (*verified*).

The open question sits in **API Terms §3.1 item 26: "Use the Content or the APIs to automate posting on the LinkedIn Services"** ([API Terms](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/l/api-terms-of-use)) (*verified*). Neither "automate" nor "posting" is defined. The item has no words limiting it to scheduled or unattended posting, and no exception for an action the account holder starts (*verified*). The plain reading treats one command the user runs as not automation. The literal reading treats any API-driven post from a CLI as automated posting. Two documents could decide it, and neither was fetched: the Developer Documentation, which yields to the Terms (§1.3), and any Additional Terms for vetted programs, which control over the Terms in a conflict (§1.2) (*verified as to precedence; contents unverified*). No LinkedIn page on user-run posting turned up (*absence unverified*). The earlier note labeled single posts, likes and comments "Allowed (plain reading)." The verification pass found no text supporting that exclusion, so those rows are **Gray** here. Unattended posting stays **Prohibited** on the literal reading, because it is the clearest case the clause names (*inference from verified text*).

Storage is the second constraint. **API Terms §4.1** bars capturing, copying, caching or storing "any Content," "except to the extent expressly permitted by these Terms or any applicable Additional Terms" (*verified; the earlier quote cut this exception*). §4.2 permits storing OAuth tokens (*verified*). §4.3 permits storing profile data only where law requires it and the member has legally valid consent, and it limits refresh of **Profile Data stored under that section** to active use (*verified; this narrows the earlier reading, which applied the limit to all profile data*). Section 1.3 defines Content as data "from the LinkedIn Services or accessed via the APIs," and the text reviewed has no carve-out for a member's own posts or profile (*verified*). [Restricted Uses](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/restricted-use-cases) sets **48-hour and 24-hour storage windows** for Marketing API member data (*verified*); whether those windows reach Share or OIDC data is unverified. UA §2.2 bars sharing the account, and UA §8.2 item 1 bars using "another's account," which concerns another person's session rather than a user's own (*verified*).

The enforcement cases target scraping at scale and fake accounts. The hiQ matter closed in December 2022 with a consent judgment and a **$500,000 judgment against hiQ**, and the settlement creates no binding precedent, according to a [Proskauer post](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/) (*secondhand*). LinkedIn says it **"has successfully resolved its lawsuit against Proxycurl"** ([LinkedIn news](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/LinkedInWinsLegalBattleToProtectMemberData)) (*verified as LinkedIn's own statement*). The injunction's terms come from a [Social Media Today report](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wins-legal-case-data-scrapers-proxycurl/756101/) (*secondhand*). In 2026, **ProAPIs agreed to a consent judgment** over mass scraping and fake accounts, per [The Record](https://therecord.media/linkedin-wins-court-order-blocking-mass-scraping) (*secondhand; docket not checked*). None of these reached a logged-in member's personal automation (*inference from the reported orders*).

The verification pass changed the earlier terms reading in four places, and this report uses its versions. The "Allowed" labels for single posts, likes and comments are withdrawn. The §4.3 refresh limit narrows to stored data. The §4.1 exception is restored to the quote. The Sales Navigator sentence on automated software, which the notes omitted, is added. Two phrases the notes relied on are not in the fetched text: "Member Data" (the defined term is "Profile Data") and "may help shape the law" (*verified*). The claim that "unofficial API" appears on none of the 12 pages holds (*verified*). The Professional Community Policies text uses "automat" three times, and those uses were not examined (*unverified gap*).

## A narrow CLI fits the open routes, and auth is the first fork

The buildable version is one ESM-only npm package that works as both a CLI and an importable library, over a thin client that exposes only in-scope endpoints. Every write is one human-triggered action, with no scheduler, daemon or batch-all command, because that is the one shape the plain reading of item 26 could protect (*design*). I would not add a local HTTP server, since it adds a listening surface and nothing the terms permit that a library call does not (*design judgment, not sourced*).

Personal or published is the largest fork. A personal tool uses one developer app and one keychain entry. A published package must make every user register their own app, because a shared secret would reach every installer. API Terms §2.2 requires keeping access credentials secret, and RFC 8252 says secrets in native apps "should not be treated as confidential secrets" (*verified*). An author-held PKCE app would avoid that, but only if LinkedIn enables it (Q4). Publishing also raises a GDPR question. Article 2(2)(c) excludes processing "by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity" ([GDPR Article 2](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-2-gdpr/)) (*verified text*). The notes did not settle whether publishing makes the publisher a controller (*unresolved; legal review*).

## Conclusion

Separating the routes changes the question. "Full control without abuse" asks for surfaces that LinkedIn approves only for partners and legal organizations, or that its clauses bar on the routes that work. The official route gives a personal tool something narrower and more useful: a signed-in identity, posting one action at a time, and, if Q2 resolves the way the Getting Access page reads, the same for reactions and comments. The existing full-coverage tools show both the demand and the design habits worth keeping, especially confirmation before any write. They also show the price of breadth. Each reaches its surfaces through cookies, a browser or a vendor's browser, and the clauses quoted above name each of those routes.

Five answers would move the ceiling. A LinkedIn statement that a user-run command sits outside item 26 would make single actions defensible. A feed-scope answer for likes and comments would move them behind Community Management approval, which the App Review page limits to legal organizations. A documented PKCE path for individuals would make a secret-free published package possible. A resolved rate-limit figure for the current posting endpoint would set the posting budget. A Community Management route for individuals would open company pages and analytics. Until those answers arrive, the narrow product is the defensible one, and the surfaces outside it stay out of scope rather than becoming targets for a workaround.
